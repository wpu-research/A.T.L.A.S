Speech-to-speech large language models return raw audio without phoneme timing, so avatar lip-synchronization pipelines that read a synthesizer phoneme schedule no longer apply. We present TranscriptionSync, a training-free three-layer pipeline: word timing is placed on the audio clock, each word interval is divided among its phonemes by articulatory-class duration weights, and fourteen mouth blendshapes are synthesized with Gaussian-kernel co-articulation and a lip-closure rule. We evaluate each layer against video-measured lip articulation (593 utterances) and in wall-clock time. When word timing comes from a streaming speech recognizer, the pipeline falls behind amplitude-driven animation, and its 95th-percentile word latency is about 3.4 seconds on natural speech; a benchmark ignoring the first-window fill time reports only 214 milliseconds. Aligning the text transcription that the language model streams ahead of its audio solves both problems: on an ordinary processor it costs 45 to 56 milliseconds per 160-millisecond step, its 95th-percentile latency on 50 live responses is 0.48 seconds, and 89% of English and 92% of Turkish word onsets fall within 100 milliseconds of forced alignment. The complete pipeline then matches measured articulation better than amplitude-only and rate-scheduled animation in blendshape distance and jaw correlation, and comes within about 0.5% of a state-of-the-art learned model in blendshape distance, although it remains behind that model in lip-mesh distance. We also show that a widely used audio-visual synchronization metric is insensitive to offsets of up to 200 milliseconds on stylized avatars and document two pitfalls of a public corpus.
